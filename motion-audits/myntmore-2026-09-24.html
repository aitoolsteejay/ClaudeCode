Overall

The flow's biggest problem isn't taste, it's arrival. Three of its five key moments - submitting the lead form, then the wizard handing off to loading, then to results - have no transition at all. The two moments that do animate spend it on the wrong things: an infinite pulse on a static badge, and 600ms of fade-in on header text, while the actual before/after demonstration sits completely still.

CriticalJakub

The page swaps its entire view the instant the lead form is submitted

WhatThe landing/lead-gate block and the wizard/loading/results block are two sibling {condition && <div>} blocks. The moment leadData is set, React unmounts the first and mounts the second in the same tick - no cross-fade, no AnimatePresence, nothing. Framer Motion is already a project dependency; it's just unused here.

Why it mattersThis is the single highest-intent moment in the funnel - someone just handed over their name, company, and LinkedIn URL. The very next thing they see is a hard cut, which reads more like a page reload or an error than a reward for converting.

Recommended motionCross-fade the two blocks with a state-driven CSS transition rather than a keyframe (the state can be entered from either direction): the outgoing block drops out over ~160ms, the incoming block enters at ~260ms with opacity + 14px translateY, ease-out. Exit stays quicker and subtler than enter.

The primary CTA's hover lift and shadow bloom snap instead of easing

WhatThe hero button variant declares hover:scale-[1.02] hover:shadow-xl hover:shadow-primary/30, but the shared base class only sets transition-colors - which covers color/background/border, not transform or box-shadow. Both "Audit My Profile" CTAs on this page use this exact variant.

Why it mattersThe design intent is right there in the class list - a subtle hover lift. It's the least-noticed kind of bug: the button visibly changes on hover, it just snaps instead of easing, so it reads as slightly broken rather than intentionally quick.

Recommended motionExtend the base class's transition property to cover what the variants actually animate - e.g. transition-[color,background-color,box-shadow,transform] at duration-200 ease-out, matching Jakub's rule that even small hover transitions feel more polished than instant.

Emil Kowalski - Restraint & Speed

Secondary lens

Problem

What's working well
  • ✓Form inputs in LeadGate and ProfileWizard don't animate on every keystroke - no motion tax on the highest-frequency interaction in the flow.
  • ✓No animation on keyboard-driven focus states beyond the standard focus ring - keyboard users aren't taxed with extra motion they didn't ask for.
Issues to address
CriticalEmil

The hero badge dot pulses forever and ignores prefers-reduced-motion

WhatThe hero badge next to "Free AI Profile Audit" puts Tailwind's animate-pulse on its dot - an infinite opacity loop with no way to pause it. Separately, the sitewide prefers-reduced-motion block only lists specific custom class names (.hero-fade, .card-fade-up, etc.) - it doesn't cover Tailwind's own animate-pulse/animate-fade-in utility classes, so this loop (and the header's fade-in) run unconditionally regardless of the visitor's OS setting.

Why it mattersPer the anti-checklist, a looping pulse on a status dot is one of the most recognizable "AI-generated UI" tells, flagged on sight regardless of frequency - and the dot isn't even indicating a live or changing status. Combined with the missing reduced-motion coverage, it's also a straightforward accessibility gap on a page that otherwise has none.

Recommended motionDrop the infinite pulse - replace it with a calm one-shot arrival (opacity + scale, ~200ms, fires once on mount, then holds still). Add .animate-fade-in, .animate-pulse (and any other Tailwind animation utilities in use) to the prefers-reduced-motion selector list in globals.css so this class of gap can't recur elsewhere on the site.

Issues to address
ImportantJhey

The page's one deliberate moment is spent on static header text instead of the actual demo

WhatThe hero's text wrapper (badge + headline + subhead) carries animate-fade-in at 600ms, while the before/after demo card directly beneath it - the literal product demonstration, the single most characteristic thing on this page - renders with no entrance treatment at all.

Why it mattersA page should spend its one deliberate motion moment on the most characteristic thing in the subject's world, not on generic label text. Right now the budget is backwards: static copy gets 600ms of motion it doesn't need - a headline needs to be readable immediately, not earned - while the "here's what you get" moment gets nothing.

Recommended motionDrop animate-fade-in from the text wrapper - headline and subhead should render instantly. Move the one deliberate moment to the demo card: since it's a genuine two-step sequence (before, then after - not an arbitrary list dressed up as one), a short sequenced reveal earns its place here: the "Before" card settles in first, the arrow follows, then the "After" card materializes with its gold border, ~90ms apart, ~220ms each, ease-out.

Which lens carried this audit

Referenced most

Jakub Krehel - Production Polish

The page's gaps are mostly craft gaps, not taste gaps: state changes that snap, a hover state that's declared but doesn't run. That's Jakub's territory - finishing what's been started - so his lens carried the ordering. Emil caught the one thing production polish alone wouldn't (the pulsing dot ignoring reduced motion); Jhey named the one place worth spending delight.

  • Lean EmilTreat every accessibility gap as non-negotiable. Audit every Tailwind animation utility against the sitewide reduced-motion list, not just custom classes.
  • Lean JakubAudit every conditional render in the funnel for a missing transition. Pair every declared hover/interactive state with a transition that actually covers it.
  • Lean JheyPick the single highest-emotion moment - the before/after demo - and over-invest there once, rather than spreading motion thin across the page.
